# How to measure a reference garment

The trick is to pick **garments you already own and like the fit of**, then send the numbers to the seller. They'll match the chart on their listing to those numbers and tell you what size to order.

## What to measure (for your haul)

For the items in your current haul, you only need 4–5 reference pieces:

1. **Fitted button-up shirt** — covers all shirts, henleys
2. **Knit / sweater you like** — covers TR Knit, Lem light grey, ribbed zip-ups
3. **Blazer or unstructured jacket** — covers TR Blazer, Lem brown blazer
4. **Pants you wear most** — covers Yohji, Lem trousers, TR pants, ERD pants
5. **Shorts** — covers TR shorts (a) and (b)

Optional but useful:
6. **Tee** if you have a fitted one whose fit you like — covers ERD shirt, Givenchy tee, etc.

You don't need to measure shoes (size is locked at 26.5 cm = EU 41), eyewear, ties, bag — those don't have fit charts.

## How to measure (universal rules)

1. Lay the garment **flat on a hard surface** — no bunching, no stretching.
2. Smooth seams. For a shirt, button it up so the front lays flat.
3. Use a **soft tape measure** (not a metal one — they kink).
4. Measure in **inches** OR **cm** — pick one and stay consistent. Sellers convert easily either way.
5. Round to the nearest **0.25 inch** or **0.5 cm**.

## What to measure per garment

### Shirts / tees / henleys / knits

These four numbers cover ~95% of what sellers ask for:

| Measurement | How |
|---|---|
| **Pit-to-pit (chest)** | Lay flat, buttoned. Tape from the bottom of one armhole seam to the bottom of the other, across the chest. Read the *one-sided* number. |
| **Length** | From the *top* of the back collar (where collar meets the back panel) straight down to the bottom hem. |
| **Shoulder seam** | From one shoulder seam (where it meets the sleeve) across the back to the other shoulder seam. |
| **Sleeve** | From the shoulder seam to the cuff edge. For raglan-sleeve garments (no shoulder seam), measure from the center back of the collar to the cuff. |

Bonus for fitted/tapered shirts:
- **Hem width** (sweep) — across the bottom hem, pit-to-pit style.
- **Cuff** — width of the cuff at the wrist.

### Pants

| Measurement | How |
|---|---|
| **Waist** | Lay flat, button up. Measure across the top waistband from edge to edge. *Double it* to get the full waist circumference. (Most sellers list waist as the full circumference.) |
| **Front rise** | From the top of the waistband at the front, down to the bottom of the crotch seam. |
| **Inseam** | From the crotch seam down the inside leg to the bottom of the hem. |
| **Leg opening** | Across the hem at the cuff, edge-to-edge. (Sellers list this as half — what you measure flat — or full circumference, varies by chart.) |

Bonus:
- **Thigh** — across the leg, 1 inch below the crotch seam, edge-to-edge.

### Shorts

Same as pants but inseam is short:
- Waist (flat × 2)
- Front rise
- Inseam (typically 5–9" for shorts)
- Leg opening (flat)

### Blazer / jacket

| Measurement | How |
|---|---|
| **Pit-to-pit** | Same as shirts — under the armholes, across the chest. |
| **Shoulder** | Seam to seam across the back. |
| **Length** | Top of back collar to hem. |
| **Sleeve** | Shoulder seam to cuff. |
| **Neck opening** | For zip jackets — across the collar opening when the zip is closed. |
